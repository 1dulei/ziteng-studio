Why include a token I'm not calling a scam? Because a scanner that calls everything a rug is useless. Most tokens are not rugs. The job is to separate the two, and that only means something if I'm willing to say "this one looks fine" when it does.

Method — what I actually check

  • Deployer track record — how many tokens has this wallet created? A count in the hundreds is a minting operation, not a team.
  • Entry-time clustering — do the top holders' first buys land in the same few seconds? Independent buyers do not synchronise.
  • Bundler / sniper flags — wallets that bought via bundle or in the first blocks, and what share of supply they hold.
  • Dev-team wallets inside the top holders — and whether they have started selling.
  • Sell behaviour vs. hold behaviour — who has taken money off the table while everyone else is still holding.
  • Liquidity against market cap — what you could realistically exit with.
